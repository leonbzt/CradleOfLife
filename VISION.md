# Cradle of Life — Game Vision Document

> *Title: **Cradle of Life**. Central concept: your **Tree of Life**.*

> **An idle RPG about evolution. You are a Player grinding a lineage up the tiers of a living, deep-time Earth — and the lineage *becomes what it grinds*. Mutations surface from where and how you grind; you draft and select what sticks; the accumulated genome is your character. Idle earns the full baseline; active play is where the richest of it lives. The world is balanced and patched by the Devs — natural selection — and your Tree of Life never finishes and never resets. It just keeps climbing, one expansion at a time.**

Mobile-first, **idle · RPG · growth**, built by a small independent team for long-term live operation. You don't play one mortal creature — you play its whole **Tree of Life**: a clade you grow, branch, and specialize across real-world time. Each branch is a character whose *biology is its build*; the rarest beneficial mutations are a brutal, never-ending chase.

---

## 1. The fantasy

*I am playing the only game that's been running since the first cell. My save is a Tree of Life; each branch is a creature whose body is its build. I grind a part of the living world and my lineage adapts to it — mutations surface, I keep the ones that fit, and over time the creature becomes what it grinds. The rarest mutations are a savage chase; a lucky innovation lets a lineage outgrow its niche and climb to the next age — while the Devs keep shipping patches that reshape the world out from under me.*

The lens is **TierZoo / r/outside**: an organism's existence read as an RPG with stats, a build, a zone, and a tier list. The unit of play is the **lineage, not the animal** — which is what lets a character grow for years without a reset. The frame is taken literally (§2), and the underlying biology is real (§2) — that rigor is what makes the content moat genuine (§17) and the educational hook authentic.

## 2. The frame — the Game of Life, literally

**The substance is life and evolution; the frame is the flavour that makes it legible and funny.** The conceit: *life is a game, it always has been, and you're playing it.* This is the literal premise, not a metaphor the UI gestures at — but it is *seasoning on a real subject* (deep-time biology), not the foundation.

- **You are a Player** — not a god, not a scientist. A player with a save file, grinding a lineage up the tiers of a living world. Your save is your **Tree of Life**; your characters are your **lineages**; your grind is **survival**.
- **The Devs are natural selection.** The world is balanced, patched, and occasionally gutted by an unseen authority you only hear from through **Patch Notes**. A mass extinction is a server-wide nerf; an oxygen catastrophe is *"Patch 2.4: atmosphere reworked; most anaerobic builds are now non-viable."* Landfall is an expansion drop. The Devs never explain and never apologize. This is the single best tonal engine in the game.
- **The world is mythic Earth, scientifically grounded.** Real ages, biomes, and ecological pressures — Cambrian shallows through the conquest of land and beyond — rendered as a retro-pixel saga. Every adaptation, stat, and trade-off reflects how real organisms actually work; the wit comes from presenting *accurate* biology as game balance — the joke is funny because it's true. Stylistic license governs naming and presentation, never the mechanics. This rigor pays off three ways: it makes the orthogonality moat *real* (§17), it gives an authentic educational hook, and it constrains AI-assisted generation to a checkable ground truth.

**Why the frame earns its place** (it is mechanism, not decoration): the while-you-were-away summary and every notification are **Dev dispatches**, so the live-ops voice *is* the fiction (§16); ages become **expansions** and extinctions become **patches**, so the multi-year roadmap is, in-fiction, the Devs' roadmap; and it is infinitely extensible — there is always a next patch.

### 2a. The voice

Wry, knowing, competent, accurate — a sharp strategy-game player narrating four billion years of real evolution as a grind. Dry wit, never slapstick; the humour is the *frame* (treating brutal, factual biology as balance decisions), never invented zaniness. Two dialects — **TierZoo** ("S-tier," "underrated pick," "this build got nerfed," "the meta") and **r/outside** ("the Devs," "RNG," "grinding," "patch notes") — and earnestness breaks through exactly once per age, at the summit, deadpan: *"Patch: Terrestrial Access is live. No build has ever stood here. Good luck."* The voice is also a **production tool**: it is the spec AI writes flavour text against, kept honest by the `bible/`.

## 3. The three pillars — in order

**idle · RPG · growth.** Everything serves these, in this priority; when two desires conflict, the earlier pillar wins.

- **Idle** — the game plays itself while you're away and rewards your return. Earnings never depend on your attention (§12).
- **RPG** — what's on screen is a character sheet, an equipment paper-doll, trained skills, and a genome — not a number ticking up.
- **Growth** — everything accumulates and nothing ever resets. No prestige wipe, no completion endpoint. The *pull* is the chase and the climb (§5).

## 4. The core insight

**You attach to the Tree, not to a beast.** This is the keystone, and it's what dissolves the usual idle/RPG/growth conflict. A mortal organism dies and resets — it can't carry monotonic growth. A bare number can't carry RPG identity — there's no character to love. A **lineage** carries both: it persists across deep time, *expresses* as a current organism you gear, **branches** into new lineages, and accumulates without bound.

Speciation comes in two biological flavours, neither a reset:

- **Anagenesis (vertical):** a lineage improves its own form — weak teeth become super teeth. The upgrade treadmill.
- **Cladogenesis (horizontal):** spin off a daughter lineage into a new niche. This is both the "new character" *and* the **respec valve** — a mis-built lineage is never punished; you branch a fresh one. Daughters inherit the parent's genome and diverge (descent with inheritance, §14).

## 5. Where the tension lives

This game deliberately removes most of the levers idle/RPG games use to create stress: **no prestige reset, no loss, no catch-up penalty, no loss-aversion, no completion endpoint, and no artificial throttle on decisions.** Each removal is a choice for a calm, no-babysitting feel. But a game with no tension is a screensaver, so the tension must be named and protected. It comes from four sources, in order of load:

1. **The chase — the engine.** There is always a rarer mutation one roll away, and the odds are brutal. The single most load-bearing system in the game; its *shape* is locked, not a tuning dial (§9a).
2. **Opportunity cost.** Accrual runs while you're away — but there's always a branch you're *not* growing, a niche you're *not* in, an age you *haven't* reached.
3. **Affix-key gates.** Binary, legible access pressure — no tundra without cold-resistance; you can *see* the wall and the key (§11).
4. **The age-climb.** Deep time pulls forward; each new age opens new niches, affixes, and a higher ceiling. Open-ended — there is no top.

> **The locked consequence:** with no completion endpoint, *the chase and the climb are the entire long-game pull.* If the chase curve goes flat, the game dies. This is why §9a's curve is structure, not a dial.

## 7. The lineage as an RPG character

What makes this read as RPG and not a bare incremental is the surface: a character sheet, an equipment paper-doll, trained skills, and a genome. The organism *is* the character; its biology *is* the build.

**Attributes** (exact list is an open dial, §20): **Vitality** (HP/size), **Power** (attack/forage strength), **Resilience** (defense/mitigation), **Metabolism** (action speed, regen, idle efficiency, and the **expression/burst budget**, §9d/§12), **Instinct** (crit, accuracy, rare-find).

**The equipment doll** is the screen the player lives on during a check-in — a paper-doll of body-part slots (Mouthparts · Integument · Locomotion · Sensory · Metabolic Core · Gland), each socketing adaptations as gear. Every adaptation carries a **tier** (weak → super), **affixes**, and a **rarity colour**. The doll is **a body, not a stat-dump**: genes express only onto anatomically valid organs (venom on mouthparts/glands, plating on the integument), and expression is **bounded per organ** (§9d) — so an organ is venomous *or* armoured, not everything at once. The signature pair is **Splice** (take a foreign gene) → **Express** (switch a banked gene on as a trait). Organ names evolve with the build (a tier-3 venom mouthpart reads *Venomous Great Frontal Appendage*).

**Role emerges from the doll — there is no class system.** What an organism *is* — predator, grazer, filter-feeder — reads off the adaptations it builds and the niche it owns, the RuneScape way (roles emerge from what you train, not a class picked up front). v1 shows a **derived role** computed from the doll; there is no chosen-class tree.

**Skills carry progression and gating; the ability-tree carries uniqueness** *(after the core loop)*. Skills train OSRS-style and gate content (Foraging, Hunting, Fortitude, Metabolism, Instinct; more as ages open) — *level the skill, not the node* — and give a guaranteed-progress floor every check-in. Later, a lineage commits down **one branch** of an ability tree (passive abilities first, then a few simple actives), earning the abilities that — with the selected genome and the owned niche — make a lineage *yours*, since gear trends toward convergent best-in-slot. Convergence is about **trait motifs** (many predators share a weapon, not a whole body), so uniqueness survives in the *combination*. Sequence: **skills → ability-tree → active abilities** (the last needs combat depth, §15/§19).

**Could there be classes? As a horizon possibility, not a v1 system.** `[horizon]` The TierZoo/RPG instinct that a lineage *is* an archetype (apex predator, armored grazer, filter feeder) is a strong identity hook. v1 derives that role from the doll and carries it through the ability-tree and the owned niche; a future patch could make archetypes a more explicit lens — a chosen identity, a tier-list badge, archetype-flavoured bonuses — *if* play shows role-from-doll + ability-tree isn't enough to make lineages feel distinct. It may equally be solved by deepening those. Named so the identity space stays open; not built, and never a class-tree by the back door.

## 8. The core loop — one verb, RPG-resolved

**Work a node** is the only loop, reskinned per trophic strategy. Two halves, RuneScape-style:

- **Forage** — gather **materials** from a node (the gather skill).
- **Metabolize** — spend materials to build and level **adaptations**, with branching outputs: the same matter becomes a shell *vs* a toxin *vs* a fat reserve, the way ore becomes a weapon *vs* armour.

**It resolves as an RPG check from the first second.** Roll your **Power vs the node's defense** → a drop (materials always; a mutation on the jackpot roll). Because even a passive node resolves with a defense check and a drop, foraging *feels* like combat-and-loot — the RPG juice is in the core verb, not bolted on. (The equip moment only lands when there's a **defended node to feel the difference against**; the first build must include one.)

**Eat vs Fight is the same verb at two kinds of node:**

- **Eat** → passive sources (plankton, detritus). Materials, low risk, reliable.
- **Fight** → living, defended prey. Materials **plus the best genes**, plus the **splice** chance (§9c), higher risk.

Fight is *not* a second engine — it is the one loop aimed at things that hit back. A dedicated combat *encounter* with abilities is deferred depth (§15, §19).

**One loop, chained into a web.** Niches yield different **materials**; metabolizing chains them (raw → refined → adaptation); stronger builds open deeper niches. The joy the design leans on is **using resources creatively and efficiently** — the build journey is an optimization puzzle, which is why a "solved" best-in-slot is fine: the joy is the efficient *path*, not the destination. Lineages specialize in parts of the web and **synergize** — but synergy is always *upside*, never a required supply chain that could starve a lineage (that would break idle-earns, §12).

## 9. The chase — mutation under selection

**Evolution is mutation under selection, and that is the engine of the whole game.** Your lineage throws up **mutations** (random variation, *biased by where and how you grind* — grind the cold and cold-adaptations surface); you and the niche **select** which stick; the accumulated **genome** is the lineage's growth, identity, and fuel. *The lineage becomes what it grinds, and a rare innovation lets it outgrow the niche and rise.* This must be **legible in-game**: the player watches cause (where you grind) become effect (what the lineage becomes).

**Two distinct paths to a gene — deliberately not the same:**

- **Mutation (vertical · common · the engine).** Your own lineage's variation, surfaced by every grind and biased by the activity. You **select** what to fix. The cozy frequent stream, and the reason the lineage becomes what it grinds.
- **Splicing (horizontal · rare · targeted).** Eat a creature and *rarely* absorb its signature trait (§9c). Stolen-and-specific, not your-own-and-emergent.

### 9a. The chase curve — locked as a shape

The most load-bearing system in the game. Its constants are tunable; its **shape is locked**:

- **Two tiers.** A frequent, cozy stream of **small mutations** keeps *every check-in eventful*, sitting under a **brutal legendary chase** (1-in-many — the long pull that justifies idle).
- **The curve never flattens.** "Expected interesting events per check-in" stays roughly constant across the whole arc, and a legendary is always reachable-but-never-given. Three mechanisms hold this without authored volume: **ages raise the ceiling** (new pinnacle genes, §15); **breadth opens fresh tables** (every new niche restarts a chase, §14); **splicing makes every new target a new hunt** (§9c).
- **No flat zone is the locked property.** With no completion endpoint, a flat curve is fatal. Protect this above almost everything.

### 9b. Two reward tiers by design

Frequent **small mutations** (steady, cozy) under a **brutal legendary chase**. Rarity decides whether a drop is a small stacking affix or a whole new capability. Effects stack, and stacking is where build depth lives.

### 9c. Splicing — the signature mechanic

The thing that makes loot feel *evolutionary* rather than RPG-with-a-skin: **eat a creature, and you can absorb one of its adaptations** — real horizontal gene transfer / endosymbiosis. Beat the venom-toad enough and you roll a chance to gain **Venom** yourself. Splicing means the world's creatures are your gene catalogue: the monster you grind *is* the upgrade you're hunting. It turns "what should I fight" into a build decision and generates the best player stories ("I spliced frost off the deep thing and now my grazer one-shots the tundra"). The targeted path — distinct from mutation's emergent stream.

### 9d. Selection is a draft against a budget

Surfaced mutations are **drafted**, not auto-kept: you choose which to express and which to cull. Two budget gates make the choice bite, while everything else stays additive:

- **Bounded expression per organ** — each organ holds only a few expressed genes (§7).
- **Metabolic upkeep** — expressing a gene costs metabolism, so a draft spends against a pool. *You can't keep all.*

So the chase is for the gene that **completes a combination**, not merely the rarest one. **Chromosomes — multi-affix genes you slot, PoE-jewel-style** — make the hunt a set-assembly puzzle (venom + metabolism, frost + ambush), and discovering that a combination stacks absurdly is a *reward*, not a bug (§11). *(When/at what level drafting and chromosomes enter is an open pacing call, §20.)*

> *Honesty constraint (§2): mutation-under-selection is truer and more legible than loot-drops, but it is still a game-ified model — you don't really choose your mutations, and real selection is brutal culling, not gentle picking. Frame it as "thematically true," never literally rigorous.*

## 10. The economy — one pipeline

Not three competing economies. One:

> **Grind a part of the web → mutations surface (biased by the activity) + materials accrue → draft & fix what sticks → metabolize materials to express it → the stronger build opens deeper web.**

Mutations and splices unlock *what's possible*; your skills/niche gate *what you can reach now*; metabolizing materials is *how you pay*. Longer-term this is an **internal, open-math economy substrate** — surplus, specialization, optimization, with the numbers shown (theorycraft is a feature, not a leak) — single-player first, the safe rehearsal for any future trading layer (§18). `[horizon]`

## 11. Gates & matchups — power, keys, light affinity

- **Power is the spine, but power is *access*, not throughput.** Clearing a node's defense *opens* it; *which stat pays out* is the niche's signature — metabolism in open water, resilience in the armored shallows, the gene-chase at the reef. A stronger generalist can *go* anywhere but *laps* no one; the specialist owns its stream at home. **Niches need teeth — steep, but never a penalty:** a build is clearly best where it fits and merely *fine* elsewhere, never dead weight (an off-home lineage must still earn idly, §12). The gap is felt as opportunity cost, never loss.
- **Affix-keys gate access** — binary and legible (no tundra without cold-resistance). Real ecological gates — thermoregulation, osmoregulation, oxygen tolerance — where the science backing pays off in legibility.
- **A clean, sequential niche/node progression ladder.** Within a niche, nodes unlock in a visible sequence (the way Melvor goes copper → iron → mithril and IdleOn works through maps), showing only the next couple of locked nodes so there's always a next thing and never an overwhelm. This is half the legibility cure.
- **Light affinity only** — soft multipliers as texture (stab/slash/crush, elemental weakness). **No hard balanced triangle** (it removes decisions and carries a permanent balancing burden).
- **No trap builds, synergies celebrated.** The target is *no strictly-dominated builds* — every path has a top-tier line, nothing is a trap — but spikes and synergies are allowed and *celebrated*. Discovering that venom + metabolism stacks absurdly is a reward, not a bug to patch (though the Devs may *say* they'll patch it, and never do). This also slashes the balancing burden, critical for a small team.

## 12. The idle & active model — *idle earns; active is the ceiling*

This is structural, not a dial. **Idle keeps the idle pillar #1; active carries the engagement** — a check-in-only loop with nothing to *do* read as dull, so active play is **core, not a sprinkle**.

**Idle is the complete floor.** Lineages earn the full baseline offline — always sufficient, never a loss, never decaying, never babysitting-required, never moving backward. You never log in because you're *losing* by being away; that loss-aversion stress is the banned anti-pattern.

**Active is a bounded, genuinely-fun ceiling — and the primary way engaged players play.** Active play is *more efficient and more fun*, but the edge is **capped and modest**, and the gap is felt as **opportunity cost, never loss or decay**. People play actively because it is the best part of the game, not to avoid falling behind.

> **The litmus test for every active feature:** *if a player never touches it, is the game still complete and are they still progressing well?* Yes → it's a ceiling; ship it. No → it's a §12 violation; cut it.

**Two axes of active play** `[direction]`: **Axis 1 — optimize the loop** (set up each lineage's activity and tune its build against a budget; it auto-runs at the idle floor) and **Axis 2 — light combat-babysit** (idle auto-resolves a fight at the floor; actively you eat/heal, pre-buff, and pop an ability for a better, capped outcome). Gene-**drafting** (§9d) rides Axis 2.

**The spike contract** (so big multiplicative combos stay safe): active may spike **materials / build speed / capture** into large multiplicative bursts — governed by the soft cap (§14) and by over-time accrual; it moves the **chase odds only modestly**; and **nothing on the floor is active-exclusive** (an idler reaches everything over time). **Bursts are phasic, not the norm** — rare, resource- or milestone-gated highs we don't balance around, so there's no always-comboing treadmill or burnout. When bursts arrive they're fuelled diegetically (metabolism / energy / food) and paced by charge/cooldown — never an energy *gate*.

**Attention is the decision layer, never throttled.** A check-in is choices — what to grind, what to chase, what to splice, what to draft, when to branch — and players make as many as they want per session. The roster is a portfolio because parallel lineages accrue in parallel, not because we ration decisions. We do **not** dress a per-session cap up as a mechanic. You start with ~2 active lineage slots and unlock more over time (paced by content, never by a stress mechanic) — all upside, never a penalty.

## 13. The roster — parallel lineages

Your Tree is a **party** of lineages — an aggressive hunter, a resilient survivor, a fast opportunist — different builds with **different attention rhythms** (a low-touch open-water farmer vs a high-touch reef hunter), all earning idly in parallel, each its own character to check in on. Over time the roster becomes an **interdependent ecosystem** where lineages synergize — but the web is a **graph of buffs, never a chain of required inputs** (synergy is upside; a hard supply chain would starve a lineage and break §12). `[horizon]`

**v1 is animals only.** Plants/fungi/microbes as full kingdoms are a 2–4× content multiplier that fights the small-team moat (§17); **kingdoms are the first major expansion**, not v1. Within animals there is already a full role spread — ambush predator, armored grazer, fast scavenger, toxic specialist, filter feeder — enough for a complete, varied roster. `[horizon]`

## 14. Growth & graduation — endless, with shape

**Monotonic growth, forever. No reset, no completion endpoint.** Endless growth still needs a shape, or old content trivializes and numbers explode. Two coupled rules:

- **Growth is breadth-led.** A single lineage's power has gentle diminishing returns near its terminal tier (a **soft cap**), so total progress comes mainly from *breadth* — more lineages, niches, ages. Per-lineage numbers stay bounded; the Tree grows without limit; and players are pushed to branch (which restarts fresh chases, §9a) rather than over-invest one hero.
- **Descent with inheritance — the Tree is the prestige layer, with no reset.** When you branch, the daughter *inherits* the parent's genome and diverges, so the Tree's accumulated history makes each new branch climb faster. This is the only "prestige" the game has and it deletes nothing: the parent persists (running or graduated, your choice), and the multiplier is the Tree's own history. It gives the satisfying "hit your cap, start again stronger and faster" loop *without* a reset.

**Milestones come from ages, not completion.** The felt structure is the **age-climb** (§15): each age is a celebrated *arrival*, not a finish line. "Reach the next shore" replaces "complete the Tree," and never runs out.

**Graduation is yours to choose.** When a lineage reaches its terminal tier, *you* decide whether to graduate it: it locks its spec, drops to low-attention passive mode, and **stays a named, permanent part of your Tree forever.** You keep your hero; you're never pushed to retire one. (Later, graduated lineages become living fixtures of the world — §15. `[horizon]`)

## 15. The world & the climb

- **The wild** — an always-present **authored baseline** of organisms per niche: your nodes, prey, and bosses, each grounded in real biology (§2). *(Later: your graduated lineages overgrow and augment them. `[horizon]`)*
- **Ages are expansions** — sea → land → onward — that unlock new niches and affixes and raise the chase ceiling (§9a). No wipe; an age is an expansion, not an extinction. In-fiction each age is a Dev expansion patch — this *is* the multi-year roadmap, shipped one age at a time, indefinitely.
- **Summit moments** — milestone payoffs and the felt replacement for a completion screen. The first summit is **land-fall** ("no build has ever stood here"). A make-or-break beat; over-polish it.
- **Apex challenges** — hard, gated bosses (real apex predators, accurately statted) holding the pinnacle genes that keep the chase ceiling moving.
- **Combat depth** `[horizon]` — a dedicated Fight encounter as a **light auto-battler** (idle auto-resolve + active food/heal/buff/ability + a pre-fight loadout, *not* a real-time rotation); may read as a second core loop (the skilling/combat split of Melvor & IdleOn). The payoff surface for active abilities (§7) and the home of Axis-2 active play (§12).
- **Patches & nerfs** — the Devs occasionally reshape the world. In v1 these are flavour and content gates; real-stakes world events are deferred depth (§19). `[horizon]`

## 16. The mobile product — a check-in floor with depth above it

A **phone game first** — a Melvor Idle / Idle Obelisk Miner shape: flat-but-deep systems, a **central number** (the genome), a few clear resources, one thumb. If a system can't be expressed on a phone, it's too much.

**The check-in is a core focus, not the whole product.** The 2–5 minute, one-handed loop is the **engagement floor**: open the app, read a **Dev dispatch**, make a handful of decisions, and close it — *just checking in always moves you forward* (§12). The product is **more than the check-in**: deeper active sessions (the two axes, §12) are the **active ceiling** where engaged players live, above the complete idle floor. The short loop hooks; the depth retains; neither is sacrificed — the game stays complete for the player who only ever checks in, and the depth never makes the short loop insufficient, never "log in or lose."

**Re-engagement is the chase, not a nag.** What brings a player back is idle accrual that ran, the chase that ticked forward, a rarer gene one roll out — the game *earns* the return; it does not manufacture it. **Notifications are optional, opt-in, fully disablable** convenience; in the Dev voice, good-news only, hard daily cap, suppressed if opened recently, never a "you're losing progress" framing (the §12 anti-pattern). Because accrual is closed-form, the next notable event is predictable, so a **local** notification needs no server — a nicety on a game that already retains.

**Touch-first, retro-pixel.** RuneScape's tabs, XP-drop pops, and rarity colours translate; hover tooltips do not — inspect is tap-to-open. The art identity is **retro pixel** — on-theme, feasible for a small team, and the style AI tooling assists best.

## 17. The content moat — combinatorics, real biology, a gated pipeline

Depth from **combinatorics, not authored volume** — the one moat a small team can dig. One loop × niches × affixes × tiers × genes × stacking = vast apparent content from small authored sets. But the moat is a *small set of mechanically orthogonal primitives* — **not** AI-generated volume; a thousand affixes that are really the same five with different nouns is the opposite of depth.

**Seven orthogonal affix roles** — the fixed axis, the moat; the count stays ~7, and adding one is a rare, expensive new term in `resolve()`:

| Role (`id`) | RPG | Biology |
|---|---|---|
| **Affliction** | poison stacks, bleed | venom, toxin, parasitism, disease |
| **Control** | chill, snare, taunt | cold, paralytic, grip |
| **Guard** | armour, resist | integument, toxin-resistance, aposematism |
| **Sustain** | haste, regen | metabolism, throughput |
| **Perception** | luck, crit | sensory acuity |
| **Penetration** | armour-pen | jaw force, enzymes |
| **Stealth** | untargetable, ambush | mimicry, crypsis |

*Affixes* grow freely *within* roles, distinguished by params (venom, bleed, disease are all `affliction`), never by a new role. Grow affixes freely; grow roles almost never.

**AI authors the rows; AI never authors the engine.** Mechanical distinctness and resolution logic are hand-designed and kept small. Every row passes **three gates** before it enters the build: **(1) Orthogonality** (automated, CI — each affix declares its role + the resolve term it touches; synonyms flagged); **(2) Scientific accuracy** (human, sourced against `bible/refs/`); **(3) Voice & world coherence** (against the `bible/`). Together they make AI-assisted content safe to lean on for years.

## 18. Monetization & product philosophy

A professional independent product, **free-to-play and ethically monetized**, grown over years through the patch cadence the fiction describes (§15). The no-reset design forgoes the predatory idle-F2P lever (prestige boosters + manufactured FOMO) on purpose: the product competes on depth, authenticity, and respect for the player's time — which is what makes the no-babysitting idle model (§12) credible. Monetization is light and deferred until the core is validated: cosmetic Tree skins and palettes, convenience unlocks (extra slots, a second accrual stream), an optional supporter pack — **none pay-to-win**, nothing that trivializes the chase. (The in-game gene chase is the *ethical, non-monetized* "gacha"; money never touches it.) Build the core as if it will never monetize.

## 19. Core vs deferred — the build focus

## 20. Open dials — tuning, not structure

Open and meant to be tuned in playtest; they do **not** reopen the locked structure (and the chase curve's *shape* is locked, §9a — only its constants are tunable). 1) Title — **decided: Cradle of Life**. 2) Exact attribute & skill lists. 3) Equipment-slot set (5–6 parts). 4) Per-niche / per-creature gene tables. 5) Drop-rate *constants* (the shape is locked). 6) Affix taxonomy within the 7 roles. 7) Active slot cap + unlock schedule. 8) How distinct the animal sub-roles feel in v1. 9) How RPG-explicit Fight gets before it crosses into the deferred encounter layer. 10) Soft-cap curve + graduation payoff. 11) How heavily the Dev voice is dialed up. 12) Real-creature vs stylized naming — **default decided**: real-named creatures in their real eras (strict chronology); invented, clearly-stylized creatures may fill gaps, contradicting no known biology. 13) The active efficiency cap, the burst peak, and the draft/chromosome entry timing (§12, §9d) — *open*.

## 21. Build disciplines — how this vision becomes a game

This document is **a guideline and inspiration source, not a build spec.** The build stays ruthlessly gated by ROADMAP.md / IMPLEMENTATION.md. Four disciplines keep an ambitious vision from collapsing into a doc that never ships:

1. **Each increment is a complete, fun game by itself.** Build many small, self-contained sub-games that *accrete* into the web. If a piece isn't fun before the next exists, the plan is wrong.
2. **Sequence the independent wins; never big-bang the co-dependent ones.** The near spine (mutation-under-selection · niches-with-teeth · legibility · the active verb) decomposes cleanly; the horizon (the deep web · abilities · combat · the economy · the ecosystem graph) co-depends — sequence it.
3. **The idle floor stays complete.** Depth is *optional on top of* a finished idle game (§12). The moment a check-in-only player feels hollow, idle-earns is broken.
4. **Legibility is a requirement, not polish.** Cause must visibly become effect — you watch the lineage become what it grinds. A playtester calling progress "random respec with no rhyme or reason" is a failure of this discipline.

- **The frame as flavour over substance** — substance is life & evolution; skin is the Game of Life: you're a Player, the **Devs are natural selection**, content is Patch Notes, the world is mythic Earth with TierZoo-true (legible, not pretend-rigorous) biology, tone wry & knowing (§2).
